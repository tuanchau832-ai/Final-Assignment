Alert rules

When a station raises the alarm

Limits follow the national surface-water regulation QCVN 08:2023/BTNMT (Ministry of Natural Resources and Environment, 2023). During a two-week set-up, each station also learns its own baseline, so a river that is already polluted still shows sudden changes.

pH6.0–8.5

Outside this range is the lowest quality level, D.

Dissolved oxygen< 4 mg/L

Below the minimum for level C water.

Turbidity+50%

QCVN has no turbidity limit, so a sudden rise above baseline signals suspended solids.

TemperatureLogged

Used to correct pH and oxygen readings.

Prototyping

Three prototypes, one working chain

Without real sensors, the team split the system into a prototype that works like the station, one that feels like the app and one that looks like the hardware.

What and why

Shows that a station can detect a discharge and raise an alert on its own.

How it was made

An ESP32 circuit on Wokwi, an online simulator. Sliders stand in for the pH, oxygen and turbidity probes; code is in C++.

Form and function

Reads every two seconds, applies the QCVN limits and three-reading rule, publishes readings over MQTT and sends a Telegram alert.

How it was tested

Four scenarios, ten runs each, fed with recorded Hanoi monitoring values.

Limitation

The simulator tests the detection and alert logic, not sensor accuracy in real water. Oxygen probes need frequent calibration, and real outfalls bring fouling and floods, so a field pilot is recommended.
