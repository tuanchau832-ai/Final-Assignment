Context

Rivers that work as open drains

Hanoi’s inner-city rivers, the Tô Lịch, Kim Ngưu and Nhuệ, carry the wastewater of a fast-growing city. Domestic and industrial discharges are both major sources of surface-water pollution in Hanoi (Nguyen et al., 2018), and only about 30% of domestic wastewater is treated before it reaches rivers and lakes (VnExpress, 2024).

Industrial discharges add a heavier load. Factory wastewater has been linked to high COD and BOD₅ levels in the Tô Lịch, a sign of organic pollution that strips oxygen from the water (Nguyen, 2019), and zinc and chromium in the sediments of four urban rivers pose a considerable ecological risk (Phung et al., 2024). In 2024, wastewater from the Thanh Oai Industrial Cluster failed national standards, and residents reported polluted irrigation water and skin irritation (Dai Doan Ket, 2024).

The damage travels downstream. Of 15 heavily polluted monitoring points in Vietnam’s main river basins, 13 lie on the Nhuệ–Đáy system, and Hà Nam province receives 10 to 15 waves of Hanoi wastewater each year (Báo Pháp Luật, 2021).

What Hanoi does today, and the gap it leaves
